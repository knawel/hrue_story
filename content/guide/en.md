The chronicle is the corporation's shared history. Members write it themselves: each of us remembers a piece, and together it makes the whole picture. Here's how it works and what the rules are.

## Who can write

- Anyone can read the chronicle, even without signing in.
- Only members who have been given access by an officer can write. Signing in with Discord alone does not grant access.
- If you're signed in but don't see "Add entry" in the header, ask an officer to give you access.
- Officers curate the chronicle: they approve milestones and can edit or hide any entry.

## Milestones and stories

**A milestone** is the corporation's official history: major battles, system defenses, moves, alliance changes. Milestones are the backbone of the chronicle, so an officer reviews each one. Until it's approved, a milestone doesn't appear in the chronicle — not even to its author. A rejected milestone never appears.

**A story** is a personal account: how you saw it and how you remember it. Stories publish immediately, without review. Readers see them after pressing "Show stories" and reading a warning that stories are unverified.

Not sure which to pick? An event for the whole corp is a milestone. Your personal take on it is a story.

## Summary and full text

Few people read long text to the end, so an entry has two versions:

- **Summary** — a few lines with the gist: what happened and how it ended. This is what the chronicle shows.
- **Full text** — the detailed account. It opens with the "Read full version" button.

For short entries you can leave the summary empty — the chronicle then shows the beginning of the full text.

## Date

Enter the date of the event and how precisely you remember it:

- **Day** — you know the exact date.
- **Month** — you only remember the month. Pick any day in it: the chronicle shows only the month and year.
- **Year** — same, only the year is shown.

Don't invent precision you don't have: an honest "2019" beats a made-up "March 14, 2019".

## Formatting

| You type | You get |
| --- | --- |
| `**bold**` | **bold** |
| `*italic*` | *italic* |
| `[link text](https://...)` | [link text](https://example.com) |
| `- list item` | a bulleted list |

Separate paragraphs with a blank line.

**Quotes.** Put the cursor where you want the quote in the full text and press "Add quote" — or type it by hand:

```
> It was glorious, we beat them all
>
> — Rory
```

You get:

> It was glorious, we beat them all
>
> — Rory

You can also select text you've already pasted and press the button — it becomes the quote, and you just fill in the author.

## Links and media

Below the text you can add links: a YouTube video, a zKillboard battle report or killmail, an image, and any other link. They show up as buttons under the entry.

The site doesn't store files — only links. Upload an image somewhere first (Discord or Imgur, for example) and paste the link. Images inside the text aren't shown — use the separate image field.

## Editing

- You can edit your own entries with the "Edit" link (for a story, it appears once the story is expanded). Officers can edit any entry.
- Edits go live immediately — a milestone doesn't need to be approved again.
- Nothing is erased: every save is a new revision, and the previous text stays in the archive. Officers can see the full edit history: who changed what, and when.
- An entry's type (milestone or story) can't be changed after it's created. If you picked the wrong one, ask an officer to hide it and create a new entry.
- Entries can't be deleted. An officer can hide one: it leaves the chronicle but stays in the archive and can be restored.

## Rules

- Write about what you saw yourself or heard from those who were there. For milestones, add evidence if you have it: a battle report, killmail, or video.
- When quoting someone, name them.
- Don't publish people's personal details — real names, contacts — without their consent.
- Don't rewrite the substance of someone else's story: it's the author's personal account. If you disagree, write your own story.
- Entries are signed with the name from your profile — the one you signed in with.
